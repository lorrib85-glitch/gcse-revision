## Important authority decisions

### Hippocrates — essential supporting knowledge

Issue 6 requires the Theory of the Four Humours and the continuing influence of Galen, but does **not** name Hippocrates. The supplied knowledge organiser does name him: it links him to the Four Humours, observation and note-taking, and explains that Galen built on his work.

Hippocrates is therefore **essential supporting knowledge**, not a named specification requirement. Episode 1 must still teach and assess the distinction because it prevents a high-value misconception: Hippocrates is associated with natural explanations, observation/recording and the Four Humours; Galen built on that foundation and added the Theory of Opposites. The Episode 1 architecture defines the minimum objective and evidence contract.

### Mary Seacole — enrichment hold

Mary Seacole is named neither in Issue 6 nor in the supplied knowledge organiser. Her existing Episode 10 material is retained only as an **enrichment hold**: it cannot satisfy the required Nightingale/nursing/hospital strand and must not become a core objective or assessed weak spot unless separately source-approved.
